# Red team verdict, 2026-11-13

A skeptical read of what merged into altaay-lab on 2026-11-13 and 2026-11-14, and of the twin those merges sit on. The question is whether we are deceiving ourselves. Nothing here was measured on a servo, a cell, a board, or a printed part. Nothing was bought.

Labels used below: sim pass only, calc only, CAD check only, cited, BLOCKER. A sim pass only number is a model output. A calc only number is arithmetic. Neither is a duck.

## Verdict in plain words

We know the file history, the cited datasheet windows we actually opened, and which model bugs we caught because a reviewer or a rerun tripped over them. We believe a 3S pack, a 12 V servo, a Castle set to 12.0 V, a 55 C rest, PETG knee sheets, and three limb breakers will make a walking duck. That belief is a stack of sims and assumptions. Gate 0 is open. The twin does not match a real duck, and the bill cannot be ordered as one consistent duck.

The straight-wire ranking is a study. Ahmed kept the regulator on 2026-11-13 at 10:18 PM ET (D64): "we definitely need regulator and all the nice stuff so we study it properly". The adopted rail is the Castle CC BEC Pro at 12.0 V (D63), checked by verify/check_power_limits.py. The wire is not the rail to build.

## What landed, and what not to quote

PRs 84, 83, 85, 86, and 87 merged as merge commits. PR 87's first commit, 6d8c153, logged joint current and then ran the knee heat with two bugs: a fall time stored as a list, and a thermal step of 0.5 s on current bins of 0.1 s, so four bins of every five never heated the model. The heat clocks in that commit, and the same clocks in the PR 87 description, are UNRELIABLE. Do not quote them.

Those unreliable clocks were: reading 55 C at 1.22 min, 60 C at 1.43 min, case peak 61.41 C, 32 C case peak 62.14 C, guard walk share 0.142 at 25 C and 0.090 at 32 C, guard case peaks 50.44 C and 51.20 C. History was not rewritten. Commit a0e2251, which merge 89da799 brought in with 6d8c153, is the correction: the first fall time is a scalar, and each current bin is one thermal step. The CPU rerun of that correction is the table in problems/RUNC_JOINT_CURRENT.md.

The corrected design row, 0.15 m/s typical, 10 min current, central corner, cut at 70 C, 25 C room, sim pass only: reading 55 C at 1.16 min, 60 C at 1.37 min, case peak 61.06 C, case at 49 C at 1.40 min. At 32 C the case peaks at 61.90 C and does not reach 62 C. The 55/45 guard walk share is 0.140 at 25 C and 0.090 at 32 C. Guard case peaks are 50.31 C and 51.01 C. The 32 C guard cooled over the last 10 min of the 90 min run. Right-knee motor RMS on the 60 s typical walk is 1.551 A (sim pass only).

Fixing the skip moved the 55 C clock by a few seconds. It did not remove it. The one-minute knee is still what this model prints. See the implausible-numbers section.

## Numbers that look implausible

Knee 55 C in about one minute at about 1.5 A RMS. After the bin fix the model still says 1.16 min to a 55 C reading at 1.551 A RMS (sim pass only). The unreliable draft said 1.22 min. The only published clock we cited for this servo family, the Robo9 oscillation, took on the order of 40 to 50 minutes to climb from about 48 C to 60 C (cited, THERMAL_TWIN.md). That hold is not this walk, and it does not look like one minute. Until one servo is logged on the bench, the one-minute knee is a model output we should not believe as a hardware time.

The 3.753 ohm winding. It is an assumed share of a stall line, not a measured resistance. Copper heat scales with it. If the winding is softer or stiffer, the one-minute clock moves. Calc only, and BLOCKER as a C018 fact.

The 0.85 regulator efficiency. Used when someone asks what a buck would dissipate. At the STS12 current that hypothetical is about 10 W and a case near 172 C (sim pass only, the playbook). The walk itself is modeled in dropout, at about 1.44 W and about 61 C (sim pass only). Quoting 0.85 as if Castle measured it is how a scary case temperature gets invented. BLOCKER.

The 0.30 V dropout. The whole "regulates only above 12.3 V" sentence hangs on it. Full pack is 12.6 V (calc only, 3 times 4.2 V). If the real dropout is larger, or the part shuts off, the 12.0 V set point does not mean 12.0 V at the servo for any useful part of the discharge. BLOCKER.

The 50 minute run time with no reserve. BATTERY_STUDY.md prints 50 minute sessions and 39 walking minutes for a 2S P45B (calc only). No reserve, no 3S measurement, no heater, no failed-cell margin. A home duck that plans on 50 minutes is planning on that calc.

## Model bugs found today, and what they say

16 V set point. The first 3S walks told the solver to regulate at 16 V. That is outside the cited 4.8 V to 12.5 V output. The sim then spent the walk in dropout, which looks like a wire, and the files talked as if 16 V were a Castle setting. PR 84 moved the 3S studies to 12.0 V and left the shared 2S default at 7.2 V. The behavior below the set point is still unpublished. A legal set point is not a measured regulator.

Mass patch not reaching MuJoCo. PR 85 edited the trunk inertial in memory and did not write the file. MuJoCo loaded the unpatched trunk, so the Castle-on and Castle-off walks were the same body. The identical speed and current were not a physics result. PR 87 wrote the file and the digits moved (sim pass only). Any earlier "we removed the part and the walk did not change" line from that patch has the same hole until someone checks the loaded body mass.

Thermal bins skipped. Commit 6d8c153 advanced the thermal model at 0.5 s and sampled the schedule once per step. The walk bins are 0.1 s. Four of five bins were skipped. A repeating pattern of one hot bin and four cold bins can be held at the hot value for the whole step. The fall list bug is the twin of that: the code looked fine on the walks that did not fall, and it crashes when one does. No logged walk in that set fell, so the crash did not show up in the table. The heat bug did show up, and only because the clocks were regenerated after the step was changed.

That is the point. These bugs lived in code that had already printed a confident table and a pull-request description. Passing a self-check on the walks that do not fall, or publishing a temperature to two decimals, did not prove the integrator. Other callers of twin_thermal.simulate still default to a 0.5 s step (knee_heat_chafe.py, thermal_cases.py, gate_p45b.py). Their inputs are not all 0.1 s bins, so they are not automatically the same four-out-of-five skip, and they were not re-audited bin by bin for this note. A second untested script can be wrong in the same quiet way. Treat every thermal clock that was not regenerated after a bin-width check as untrusted, including the 6d8c153 set, which is UNRELIABLE.

## Top 5 measurements

These five would cut more uncertainty than another sim pass.

1. One STS3215 bench heat and current test. Hold a C018 near the logged knee RMS, about 1.5 A, at the voltage the rail actually delivers, and log Present Temperature and current until 55 C or 30 minutes. That is the measurement that tells us whether 1.16 min is the servo or the model.
2. Castle at a 12.0 V set point, input swept from 12.6 V down to 9.0 V at a few amps. Does it hold 12.0 V, pass the input through, or shut off? That replaces the 0.30 V dropout and the 0.85 efficiency, or shows they were the wrong model.
3. Cold resistance of one C018 winding, next to a stall-current measurement at a known voltage. That confirms or throws out 3.753 ohm.
4. One limb's supply current during a real walk, as a time above 7.23 A, not as a 100 ms average. That is the blanking check for the TPS259827. Until it exists, nuisance trip is BLOCKER.
5. Rested full-pack voltage and a few minutes of pack voltage and current on a real 3S walk. That puts a clock and a voltmeter on the dropout claim and on the 50 minute calc, which has no reserve.
