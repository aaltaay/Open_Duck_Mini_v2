# Electromechanical twin: walk, 14 servos and the as-built power path in one closed loop (sim pass only)

- When: 2026-11-03 3:18 PM (America/New_York)
- Source: working tree of branch claude/digital-twin after commit 1e9888f (the lead commits); work by the electromech twin sub-agent for the lead, in altaay_lab/twin/electromech/ only
- Written: 2026-11-03 by the Claude Code agent

- What was built: twin/electromech/em_twin.py runs the stock BEST_WALK_ONNX_2 walk (the lab's copy of the upstream MjInfer loop) with each of the 14 STS3215 servos taking its gain and torque limit from its own supply volts (BAM: both scale with V / 7.4; servo_sts3215.py), the as-built power path (D15 star plus the D23 feeds SP4/SP5, netlist sha256 0f4af5bff3) parsed from the ngspice circuit of sim/power_fix.py option ab and solved by nodal analysis at every 2 ms step (power_net.py), a 2S Molicel P30B pack from the data sheet discharge curves (p30b.py, data/p30b_datasheet.json), and the D16 guard (bench/battery_guard.py, unchanged) in shadow mode. Nothing in sim/, bench/ or wiring/ was changed; they are imported read only.
- Validation: every servo at 7.4 V gives the trained actuators exactly (0.0 Nm over 16 s, trained mass and our build), and the stock twin run is the lab loop exactly (the E5 and C8 traces, 0.0 difference). The Python network against ngspice on the same 12 open-loop replays: largest servo volts difference 3.8 mV, median 0.6 mV, lowest servo identical in all 12; the closed loop's own currents fed to ngspice: 0.19 and 0.25 mV. Servo voltage scaling against the C001 data sheet (6.0 / 7.4 V): no-load speed ratio 0.807 (data sheet 0.807). P30B digitization against sim/runtime_thermal.py: 13 mV at most.
- Result 1, the sag slows the walk and does not drop it: backlash-model walk, our 2.65 kg build, share of the trained-actuator speed: full 93 / 91 % (typical / high-side corner), nominal 79 / 76 %, near the D16 cut 76 / 68 %, at 3.3 V per cell 49 / 31 %; plain walk 96 / 93, 73 / 70, 67 / 58, 43 / 39 %. No fall in 85 closed-loop runs (pushes included).
- Result 2, closed against open loop: the replay overstates the dips (a weaker servo draws less). As-built cases, lab cells, nominal, typical: right knee under 6.0 V 9.8 % open, 1.9 % closed; high-side head roll 33.5 against 25.3 %; while the walk runs at 0.73 / 0.69 of the command against 0.90 trained.
- Result 3, sessions from a full charge (no time compression): the walk falls under 85 % of its full-charge speed at minute 16.7 (backlash, typical; SOC 48 %), 15.6 (high-side) and 14.1 (plain, typical); the guard's walk stop comes after, at minute 20.2, 18.3 and 30.0. No session fell.
- Result 4, sweep: hop 28 against 40 mOhm per conductor moves the walk speed under 1 %; BEC dropout 0.10 / 0.30 / 0.60 V gives 0.752 / 0.706 / 0.663 of the command at a nominal pack (typical); a firmware that compensates its bus volts (not published) would keep 0.859.
- Outputs: twin/electromech/ELECTROMECH_TWIN.md (method, validation, tables, trace format, assumptions, BLOCKERs), out/ (JSON summaries and the session CSVs in 5 s bins for twin/thermal), two figures; full traces in the session scratch folder twinEM (session/*.npz, states/*.npz), not in git.
- Open: the servo firmware's response to its bus volts (BLOCKER; bench test proposed for TEST_PLAN 7), the BEC dropout and minimum input (TEST_PLAN 4.1), the real hop and holder (5, 3.2), the 3S plan not run in the twin, the plain-walk session in the high-side corner not run.
