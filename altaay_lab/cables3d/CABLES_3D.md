## How the routes were made [L]
- Robot posed from mini_bdx/robots/open_duck_mini_v2/robot.urdf at zero (yourdfpy, mm); printed parts from print/; head parts that exist only in print/ added in the head.stl frame; boxes for bought parts not in the CAD (audit/placements.json, viewer seats for the 5 V regulator, camera and foot switches, and the six seats chosen here).
- 1 mm occupancy grid (voxel solid when its centre is inside a watertight part or the surface crosses it), distance field, then a shortest clear path for each run (Dijkstra, clearance = bundle radius + 0.5 mm, fallbacks to 0.6 mm), string pulled. Runs follow the servo chains through the neck and legs (vias 10 mm off each servo socket), the lab corridors in the trunk and the hull of the trunk and head shells.
- Every point is bound to the URDF link of the part it rides on (dynamic programming along the kinematic chain, cost = distance to that link's parts). Where the link changes the segment is a free span across the joint (the service loop). Points are stored in link-frame mm (cables3d.json `points[].p`).
- Lengths: zero pose, and min/max over the full URDF range of every joint each free span crosses (61 samples for one joint, 21 x 21 for two). A second sweep uses the head ranges the Runtime commands from the gamepad (head pitch -0.78..0.30, yaw -0.5..0.5, roll -0.5..0.5 rad, Open_Duck_Mini_Runtime 376de65 mini_bdx_runtime/mini_bdx_runtime/xbox_controller.py lines 15 to 17) [U].
- The routes are centrelines of taut cables. They never cross a solid voxel; the grid is 1 mm, so gaps under about 1 mm are not resolved (the exact-mesh ray checks in seats_ties.py and the connector room check use the real meshes).
