## 3. Library behaviour that matters for this build

The Runtime uses two servo libraries: rustypot 0.1.0 for the walk (HWI) and pypot (pollen-robotics, branch support-feetech-sts3215, head f6d305e) for configure_motor.py, configure_all_motors.py and check_voltage.py [U setup.cfg lines 19, 25; scripts]. Both were read at those versions [I PyPI sdist rustypot-0.1.0, rustypot core c8b8223 from its Cargo.lock; github.com/pollen-robotics/pypot].

1. **rustypot 0.1.0 cannot read voltage, current or temperature.** Its Python IO exposes position and speed reads, goal writes, P, D, mode, torque and goal time only [I rustypot 0.1.0 src/lib.rs lines 18-133]. The guards therefore read through pypot, with check_voltage.py's calls.
2. **rustypot opens the port exclusively** (TIOCEXCL) [I serialport 4.7.0 src/posix/tty.rs line 131]. A second program cannot open the port while the walk runs (except as root). Our scripts open pypot first, then let the HWI open rustypot; both live in one process and take turns in the walk thread.
3. **G10 crash mechanism, reproduced.** Before each send, rustypot flushes the input buffer, then asserts that it is empty [I rustypot c8b8223 src/protocol/mod.rs lines 71-77]. If bytes are still arriving (the rest of a sync read after a checksum error, or noise), the assert panics. The panic is a pyo3 PanicException, a BaseException that the HWI's `except Exception` misses. The Mutex is poisoned, so every later call panics too. With the real rustypot 0.1.0 binary on the simulated bus, a corrupted status packet plus stray bytes gives exactly this panic [L tests/test_battery_guard.py::test_rustypot_panic_after_checksum_error_is_caught]. walk_with_guard.py catches it and turns torque off through its pypot handle (exit 3). A missing servo costs 1 s per rustypot read: its read timeout is 1000 ms [I rustypot 0.1.0 src/lib.rs line 320].
4. **pypot can discard writes that were already sent.** Before every packet pypot flushes input AND output [U pypot abstract_io.py __real_send, flush]. On the Pi's cdc-acm driver, an output flush unlinks write URBs still in flight [I raspberrypi/linux rpi-6.12.y drivers/usb/class/cdc-acm.c lines 893-904]; on a pty it drops unread bytes. pypot setters are sync writes with no reply, so the next pypot call can cancel the previous write. On the simulated bus:
   - raw pypot lost 143 of 150 rustypot P writes that were followed at once by a pypot read [L tests/test_protocol.py];
   - one run of the Runtime's own configure_motor.py printed `max_acceleration: (50,)` and `D coefficient: (32,)` after "configuring" [L run log, first_servo_check report]. That is the G1 symptom: register 85 left at the factory 50.
   How often this happens on the real adapter is UNVERIFIED (it depends on USB timing). Mitigations: our pypot handles replace the flush with drain-then-input-flush (bench/odm_bench/bus.py make_flush_safe; 0 of 150 lost [L]). first_servo_check.py reads every configured register back. `--fix-config` writes configure_motor.py's values again with the same setters and verifies each one.
5. **pypot sync read hides which servo failed.** It returns an empty tuple when any reply after the first is missing [U pypot sts3215_io.py lines 20-41]. Our `sync_read_block` keeps the replies that did arrive, per ID [L].
6. Units used: voltage 0.1 V per LSB [U check_voltage.py line 31]; current 6.5 mA per LSB, temperature 1 C per LSB [I github.com/commanderfun/STS3215 REGISTER_REFERENCE.md, fc359b0]. The sign bit of Present Current is UNVERIFIED; the gates use magnitudes.

## 4. IMU axes and mounting (B20)

- Policy frame, checked in MuJoCo on the Playground model (scene_flat_terrain.xml, imu site = trunk frame) with mj_inverse at zero acceleration [L sim pass only]: level gives accel (0, 0, +9.81); 20 deg nose down (+y rotation) gives accel x = -3.36; 20 deg right side down (+x) gives accel y = +3.36; pitch-down, roll-right and yaw-left motions give positive gyro y, x and z. So x is forward, y left, z up.
- The Runtime maps the chip to that frame with axis_remap (Y, X, Z; -, +, +), or (-, -, -) when imu_upside_down is true [U raw_imu.py lines 30-48]. On the simulated byte-level BNO055 (the real adafruit_bno055 5.4.13 driver applies the remap registers), raw_imu.Imu gives (-3.37, -0.01, 9.22) for 20 deg nose down with the mounting the remap expects [L].
- bench/imu_check.py --tilt checks those signs on the robot. Its tests show it catches a board turned 90 or 180 deg or flipped, and passes a flipped board once imu_upside_down is true [L tests/test_imu_check.py]. A board turned 90 or 180 deg in its plane cannot be fixed in duck_config; it needs a physical turn (no code change, D6).
- The walk reads only gyro and accelerometer [U v2_rl_walk_mujoco.py lines 158-159]; NDOF fusion and the magnetometer do not enter the policy.

## 5. Guards: battery (D16) and servo temperature

Both run inside the walk thread, on one staggered SYNC READ of registers 62 and 63 (odm_bench/sampler.py) [L].

**Battery (battery_guard.py).** Decision on the median of the 14 Present Voltage readings. Walk stop when the 5 s mean of the median is at or under 6.0 V while walking. Hard stop when the 3 s mean is at or under 6.0 V while standing, or the 2 s mean is at or under 5.4 V while walking. Start refused under 6.8 V idle. Lost reads for 5 s while walking also stop the walk. Mapping from the lab power model, star wiring (D15), typical corner [L bench/sim_profiles.py on sim/power_path_sim.py build(); sim pass only]:

| Cell open-circuit V | 4.0 | 3.9 | 3.8 | 3.7 | 3.6 | 3.55 | 3.5 | 3.4 | 3.3 | 3.2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Median servo V while walking (mean of 6 s) | 6.86 | 6.68 | 6.48 | 6.28 | 6.07 | 5.97 | 5.86 | 5.66 | 5.45 | 5.24 |

- Why D16 cannot be applied literally: while walking, the median reads 6.0 V at about 3.57 V per cell. That is where D16's walk stop belongs (3.5 V per cell under load, about 7.0 V at the pack). A torque-off at 6.0 V while walking would drop the robot at the walk-stop point. At light load 6.0 V is about 3.2 to 3.3 V per cell, D16's hard-stop intent, so the guard applies 6.0 V as a hard stop only when standing [L]. Replays: no trip at full or nominal charge; on the sim discharge the walk stop lands at the 3.55 to 3.6 V step and the standing hard stop near 3.4 V, with a +0.5 V standing recovery that is an estimate [L tests/test_battery_guard.py].
- In the high-side resistance corner the median at a nominal pack (3.7 V per cell) is 5.89 V. The guard would stop the walk at nominal charge [L replay]. The thresholds hang on the real path resistance: run battery_guard.py --monitor with the bench supply (PI_SETUP step 10.7) after TEST_PLAN 4 and 5.
- If D16(b)'s fallback regulator is fitted (Pololu D42V110F6, fixed 6 V), every servo reads about 6 V at any charge. The servo-voltage guard then cannot see the battery and would hard stop at once. That fallback needs a pack voltage sense first.

**Servo temperature (odm_bench/thermal.py).** Max Temperature Limit (register 13) is read from every servo before torque-on. Warn at limit - 15 C, stop at limit - 8 C, each confirmed by 2 reads of the same servo in a row. With the factory 70 C [I REGISTER_REFERENCE.md] that is 55 C and 62 C. Start refused if any servo is already at its warn level.
- Warn: the same stand sequence as the battery walk stop, then 2 s standing, then a 2 s sink with the Runtime's low gains (HWI.low_torque_kps = 2), then HWI.turn_off(). The Runtime has no sit pose: HWI has init_pos = standing and zero_pos = straight legs [U rustypot_position_hwi.py lines 33-69]. Stop: low gains 0.5 s, torque off, at once. Neither powers off. The hottest servo is logged every second (thermal_<time>.csv).
- Where the margins come from: at the limit the servo unloads itself, one joint at a time (Unload Condition register 19 = 44 includes the temperature bit [I REGISTER_REFERENCE.md]). D2 sets the lab's continuous-duty edge at limit - 10 C. The warn at limit - 15 C stops 5 C before that edge, because the reading keeps rising after a stop (holding current; the sensor is not the winding). The stop at limit - 8 C leaves room for the 1 C step, the 0.5 s read period, the 2-read confirmation and the sink. Both are lab choices. The heating rate is not published, so check them against the TEST_PLAN 7.3 log (C per second at 1.5 Nm). The sim agent's knee copper loss of 3 to 5.5 times the rated 1.06 W (coordinator note) is why this guard exists.
- Tests on the simulated bus with the real walk loop: a left knee rising 1 C/s gives warn, stand, sink, torque off (exit 7). A right knee with its own 65 C limit rising 5 C/s gives warn, then the stop at 57 C (exit 8). A knee at 58 C at start refuses to start (exit 9) [L tests/test_thermal_guard.py].

**D2 thermal test caution (TEST_PLAN 7.3).** 1.5 Nm on a C001 is about 2.0 A at its stall ratio (1.91 Nm at 2.5 A [I datasheet via SERVO_DEEP_DIVE.md]). The factory protection current is also 2.0 A (register 28 = 310 x 6.5 mA) for 2 s (register 35 = 200 x 10 ms) [I REGISTER_REFERENCE.md]. The hold may trip the servo's own unload. first_servo_check.py reports that as INCOMPLETE [L tests/test_first_servo_check.py]. A hold near the sim walking RMS (1.45 A, about 1.1 Nm, sim/CURRENT_BUDGET.md, sim pass only) stays clear.

## 6. Bus timing budget (can the 50 Hz loop afford the extra reads?)

From `python3 altaay_lab/bench/timing_budget.py`. Packet sizes as rustypot builds them [I rustypot c8b8223 src/protocol/feetech.rs]: instruction 6 + parameter bytes, status 6 + data bytes. 1 Mbaud 8N1 = 10 us per byte. The packet sizes were confirmed on the simulated bus with the real libraries: 22-byte sync read request and 50-byte goal sync write for 14 servos [L tests/test_protocol.py].

| Transaction | Bytes | Wire ms | Reads |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runtime per cycle: sync read 56 + sync read 58 + sync write 42, 14 servos | 318 | 3.18 | 2 |
| guard tick: sync read 62..63 of 7 servos, every 0.25 s (12.5 cycles) | 71 | 0.71 | 1 |
| same for all 14 at once (start check) | 134 | 1.34 | 1 |
| voltage only, 14 servos (check_voltage.py) | 120 | 1.20 | 1 |
| power log, every cycle: sync read 62..70 of 14 | 232 | 2.32 | 1 |

- Each read also waits one USB round trip through the CH343 full-speed device and cdc-acm. That time is not published (UNVERIFIED). At 1 ms per read: Runtime bus time 5.18 ms per 20 ms cycle; with a guard tick in that cycle 6.89 ms; with the power log every cycle 8.50 ms. That leaves 14.8, 13.1 and 11.5 ms for the policy and Python, whose cost on a Pi Zero 2 W is UNVERIFIED.
- Measured on the shared x86 lab machine, real walk loop and policy, wire-timed simulated bus, no USB latency [L timing_budget.py --measure]: baseline 3.4% of cycles over 21 ms; with the staggered guard 5.1% (read 1.43 ms mean); with the power log every cycle 12.1% (read 3.98 ms mean, p99 cycle 30.8 ms). The Pi is slower.
- Answer: the guard (both guards, about 0.06 ms per cycle on average) is affordable. Logging all 14 servos at 50 Hz inside the loop is marginal even on x86. power_log.py reports the late fraction (9.loop); if it warns, log with `--decimate 2` (25 Hz) and say so in the history entry. TEST_PLAN 9's "50 Hz" may need to become "50 Hz, or 25 Hz if the loop runs late".
