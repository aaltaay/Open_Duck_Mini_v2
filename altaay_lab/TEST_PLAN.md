## 1. Before any power: mechanical checks of every made cable
For each made lead in CABLE_MAP.md (P rows, the servo power leads SP1 to SP5, the data Y, D rows):
1. Crimp pull: each Molex 08-70-1040 terminal holds when pulled by hand along the wire (no slip) and sits latched in the housing (a light push on the wire does not move it back). Fail: redo the crimp.
2. Connector fit: plug every made lead into the real servo port and adapter socket it goes to; it must seat fully and latch. Fail: wrong housing or terminal.
3. Heat shrink covers every solder joint and splice with no copper showing: every splice, every solder joint on a part's flying lead (holder, BEC, resistor and LED leads), each XT30 solder cup and the cut BEC third wires (34 sleeves, BOM_FULL.md heat shrink row); joints on board pads, holes and header pins get none, and switch terminals, lugs and tabs get a sleeve only where copper shows. On each splice the 15 mm adhesive sleeve reaches at least 2 x the wire OD onto the insulation at both ends (NASA-STD-8739.4A 19.2.6), no splice sits in a bend (7.2.36), and YG3 sits about 15 mm past YD3 on the adapter H3 lead (HARNESS_BUILD.md, Splices). Twisted cables (the 'Twist' line of each cable in HARNESS_BUILD.md: SP3, SP4, SP5, P13, D2, D3, D4, D6, D10 and SD1 branch b): count the turns over 100 mm; the lay is inside the listed range, in one direction end to end, with about 20 mm untwisted at each end. Kit cables stay as shipped. Fail: copper showing, a sleeve short of 2 x OD, a splice in a bend, or a lay outside the range.
4. Length, loops and ties (the test fit): route each cable on the printed parts and dress each joint loop with the length HARNESS_BUILD.md gives between its two ties. The neck bundle makes one loop over head pitch and head yaw, with no tie on head_pitch_to_yaw. Turn every joint the cable crosses through both limits by hand (CABLE_MAP length rule). Turn head yaw to +/-45 deg (8.2b), and once, slowly, to the full +/-160 deg; stop at the first pull or kink. In CAD all 43 loops meet 3 x OD (cables3d/CABLES_3D.md). Ties (cables3d.json, 75):
   - Each MB3A (32) and ABM1M-AT-C (23) mount sits flat and holds a hand pull once the adhesive has cured.
   - Each drilled tie-hole pair (3) is deburred, and the strap runs behind the wall.
   - The 9 bundle ties (HARNESS_BUILD.md 'Bundle ties checked at the test fit': T016, T017, T023, T024, T026, T034, T045, T085, T089) keep the bundle clear of moving parts at both limits. Only if one reaches a moving part, report it: that point gets a drilled tie-hole pair or a printed anchor (D25).
   Fail: tension at a limit, a kink or fold in a loop, a cable on a moving edge, or a mount that lifts.
5. Ankle plugs at foot_side (cables3d/CABLES_3D.md; HARDWARE_AUDIT B33): on the first printed foot_side (the left leg prints first) with the TO1 servo, plug a kit cable into BUS 2 and an SP4 housing (V and G only) into BUS 1, then turn the ankle through its range with the foot flat. In CAD the plug back sits 0.5 to 1.7 mm from the foot_side apex and the SP4/SP5 route touches foot_side. Pass: no plug body or wire presses on foot_side. Fail: a 2 mm relief at the foot_side apex (CAD change) before the other plates print.

## 2. Before any power: continuity and polarity, every wire
Use the generated checklist audit/continuity_checklist.csv (one row per wire, from wiring/netlist.yaml by audit/erc.py; the netlist passes the electrical rule check with 0 errors, audit/ERC_REPORT.md): for every wire, measure from its "from" pin to its "to" pin, and check there is no connection to the pins listed in its last column.
- Read every pass as a number in ohms, not by the beep: the TE1 meter (Klein MM420) beeps for anything under 50 ohm, so a beep cannot show "under 1 ohm" and silence cannot show "over 1 Mohm". Use the resistance setting (press SEL once from continuity), touch the probe tips together first and subtract that reading. Its lowest range, 400.0 ohm, reads in 0.1 ohm steps at +/-(1.2 % + 5 digits), about +/-0.5 ohm near zero (Klein MM420 manual, https://data.kleintools.com/sites/all/product_assets/documents/instructions/klein/MM420%20Instructions_web.pdf, read 2026-11-01 9:52 PM ET).
- Pass: under 1 ohm for every wire; over 1 Mohm between any two different nets on the same connector (V+, GND, DATA on every 3-pin servo lead; P+ and P- everywhere).
- Polarity: on every servo lead the pin order matches the STS3215 port (GND, VCC, DATA per the Feetech sheet); SP4 and SP5 carry V and G only, data cavity empty (D23); on the BEC output, red is +; on the XT30, + to +.
- Pack bus to ground with the switch ON and nothing powered: over 1 kohm after the capacitors charge from the meter (no short).
- Adapter H1 screw terminal: not connected to anything (D15).

## 3. Battery, holder and BMS (bench, robot not connected)
1. Cells: both P30B within 0.05 V of each other before they go in (the Tenergy board does not balance).
2. Holder drop: 10.0 A through the holder for 30 s, from the pack (BMS fitted, P+ and P-) into the electronic load (TE4) in constant current: 74 to 84 W at 7.4 to 8.4 V, under its 200 W. The bench supply cannot run this step (TE2 stops at 8 A). 10 A is the Tenergy board's rated operational current ("10A or less"), well under its trip. Measure across the holder contacts. Pass: under 0.10 V total (10 mOhm) and no contact gets warm in 30 s. The section 9 gate needs the typical pack side (sim/POWER_FIX.md: with a 30 mOhm holder the ankle is under 6.0 V 17.7 % of the walk at nominal even with the second feed). Also read the voltage at the Pololu 2815 output at 10 A: pack open circuit minus that reading under 0.95 V (95 mOhm cells to switch). This replaces the 10 to 30 mOhm holder value in the power sim.
3. BMS: P+ to P- equals the pack voltage. Then the over-current trip, on the electronic load (TE4) only, after step 2 passes. Tenergy spec table (BOM line 8 link, read 2026-11-01 9:57 PM ET): over-current detection 22 +/- 5 A, so 17 to 27 A; detection delay 8 +/- 4 ms; release condition "Cut load"; main loop 25 mOhm max.
   - Pack at 7.40 V or less, open circuit, so 27 A stays inside the load's 200 W input rating (200 W / 27 A = 7.41 V). Above that the load's over-power protection (OPP, 210 W) can cut the current, which would look like a BMS trip. If the pack reads higher, discharge it on TE4 at 2 A and read it again after 5 minutes off the load.
   - Circuit: P+ through the fuse holder with a 15 A fuse (L3 with L4) to TE4 +, P- to TE4 -.
   - TE4 in Program (auto test) mode, CC, 30 A range, Stop On Fail on, 7 steps: 15, 17, 19, 21, 23, 25 and 27 A. Each step: Ton 0.05 s (load on), Tdly 0.03 s (when the load reads the voltage, after the longest 12 ms detect delay), Toff 2 s (load off), pass band 1.0 to 8.5 V. The sense is inverted: a failed step means the voltage collapsed, so the BMS opened at that current.
   - Pass: the 15 A step passes and the run stops on a step from 17 to 27 A; write down that current. Fail: it trips at 15 A, or all 7 steps pass (no trip at 27 A).
   - V1 pass line (2026-11-04, B47; twin/electrical/ELECTRICAL_TWIN.md, sim pass only): the board must trip at 21 A or more. In the twin a 60 N shove to the left of the standing duck draws 19.5 to 20.6 A from the pack, over 17 A for 4 to 13 ms (6 of 6 seeds); a board that trips at 20 A or more trips in 0 of 41 simulated falls and get-ups, a board at 17 A in 7. If this board trips under 21 A: a second board (BOM line 8; buying needs Ahmed's OK), or accept that a hard shove can switch the robot off (release "Cut load": cycle the panel switch).
   - With the target guard in the walk loop (2026-11-04 track 1b, B47; twin/electrical/fix/bus_budget.py, sim pass only): the twin's worst 4 ms is 15.9 A, so any board that trips inside its band (17 A or more) passes. The 21 A line applies only to a walk without the guard.
   - Heat: each step lasts 50 ms. Even if the BMS never trips, the 27 A step puts about 0.9 J into its 25 mOhm loop (27 A x 27 A x 0.025 ohm x 0.05 s) and at most 10 J into the load (7.4 V x 27 A x 0.05 s), so nothing gets hot.
   - After the run: disconnect the load (the release condition) and check P+ to P- is back at the pack voltage. Read the fuse in ohms (under 1 ohm, as in section 2); if it opened, the run does not count: fit a new fuse and repeat.
   - Load specs used here, read 2026-11-01 9:53 PM ET: 30 A range, 200 W input power, OPP 210 W, 0.9 V minimum operating voltage at 30 A (SDL1000X datasheet DS0801X-E01F, https://siglentna.com/wp-content/uploads/dlm_uploads/2024/05/SDL1000X_DataSheet_DS0801X-E01F.pdf); Program steps with Ton and Toff 0.01 to 100 s, Tdly 0.01 s to Ton, the pass band in volts in CC mode, and Stop On Fail (SDL1000X user manual E02A, https://siglentna.com/wp-content/uploads/dlm_uploads/2024/06/SDL1000X_UserManual_E02A.pdf). Both are listed on https://siglentna.com/resources/documents/dc-electronic-load/; the product page in BOM TE4 is https://siglentna.com/product/sdl1020x-e/.
   - Without TE4, skip the trip test and rely on the fuse. Do not short the pack through a resistor: the current would depend on cell and contact resistances nobody has measured, so no resistor value is sure to pass 27 A, and if the BMS failed only the fuse would end the short.
4. Charge once with the USB-C charger and the robot off: both cells end within 0.05 V of each other and at no more than 4.20 V each.
5. Power-on (B48; twin/electrical/ELECTRICAL_TWIN.md, calc only): the real pack, BMS, fuse, Pololu 2815, CC BEC Pro and 5 V regulator wired as built, no servos yet. Mate the XT30 only with the panel switch off. Switch on with the panel switch 20 times, 10 s on and 10 s off. Pass: the BMS never opens (P+ to P- stays at the pack voltage) and the BEC and the regulator come up every time. Repeat once more at TEST_PLAN 8 with all 14 servos fitted, on the pack. Reason: Tenergy publishes no short-circuit threshold or delay and Castle no input capacitance; the over-current function cannot trip at power-on (it would need about 25 mF on the bus), the short-circuit function is not known.
   - Track 1b (B48, calc only): also read the pack current on the scope through the 20 power-ons (a 10 mOhm shunt in the P- lead, or a clamp probe). Pass: never over 17 A for 1 ms or more, and never over 19 A at all (the worst cited short-circuit point of this class, TI BQ2970 family, 125 us). If it fails, fit the ready soft start (4.7 kOhm in the P15 lead, 2.2 uF from the 2815 SW pad to VOUT; parts, needs Ahmed's OK) and repeat.

This measures the dropout the power sim assumed (0.3 V). Dropout pass: output at least input minus 0.30 V at 7.0 V in and 6 A out (sim/POWER_FIX.md: 0.6 V of dropout takes the ankle from 5.8 to 20.9 % under 6.0 V with the second feed). 10 minutes at 5 A on TE4, no airflow: case under 60 C.
   - Heat check log: the case temperature every 30 s, and the input and output volts and amps at 5 A; compute the efficiency (output W / input W). Twin prediction (twin/thermal/to1_predict.json, 22 C room, sim pass only): the 60 C gate passes at 92 % efficiency (54 C after 10 minutes) and fails at the 85 % the power sim assumes (about 94 C) and at 80 % (146 C). The measured efficiency replaces the 85 % in sim/power_path_sim.py and twin/thermal. This bench case is not the robot: in the closed body, walking to the battery stop, the twin puts the case at 98 to 122 C at 85 % (HARDWARE_AUDIT B37).
   - Twin (twin/electromech/ELECTROMECH_TWIN.md, closed loop, sim pass only): at a nominal pack the walk speed follows the dropout: 0.752 of the command at 0.10 V, 0.706 at 0.30 V, 0.663 at 0.60 V (typical corner; 0.710, 0.684 and 0.595 high-side). 2. Pololu 2815: on-board slide OFF, control switch wired. Switch on: VOUT within 0.1 V of VIN at 5 A on TE4. Switch off: VOUT 0 V. Then 10 minutes at 6.0 A (TE4), switch on its seat with the foam tape: board under 50 C. Reason: as wired (D15 plus the D23 feeds, netlist lengths) the sim pack RMS current while walking is 5.61 to 5.93 A for our 2.65 kg build at full and nominal charge, up to 99 % of the 6.0 A the 2815 carries with its MOSFETs at 55 C (Pololu: 12 V, 22 C ambient, still air), and 6.04 A at the low pack (sim/POWER_PATH_SIM.md 'As built', VERIFICATION.md C7). Over 50 C: add a second 2815 in parallel below the first (40.8 mm free below the seat in CAD).
   - Heat check log: the board temperature every 30 s. Twin prediction (twin/thermal/to1_predict.json, 22 C room, sim pass only): 50 to 59 C after 10 minutes at 6.0 A with a 7.4 V input (central 55 C), steady about 63 C (Pololu's own point is 55 C at 12 V; a lower gate voltage raises the on resistance). So the twin expects the 50 C gate to fail and the second 2815 to be needed.
3. 5 V regulator: input 6.0 and 8.4 V; output 5.00 +/- 0.15 V at no load and at 2.4 A on TE4 for 1 minute.
4. Fuse holder: fuse fitted, under 10 mOhm across the holder (Kelvin method below: TE2 at 2.0 A, no load).

## 5. Kelvin resistance of one servo hop (decides D15 follow-up)
Pass 2.0 A from the bench supply through the V+ conductor of one kit cable hop, servo board to servo board (cable plus both contacts plus one servo board pass-through), and measure the voltage across it with the meter leads on the servo pins, not on the supply leads. R = V / 2.0 A. Repeat for GND.
- Power sim corners: typical 27.6 mOhm per conductor per hop, high-side 65.1 mOhm (sim/POWER_FIX.md).
- Bands per conductor, 2S pack with the second feeds SP4/SP5 (lead decision L3, DECISIONS.md D23 note):
  - Pass: at most 28 mOhm per conductor. Gate: 28 mOhm per conductor. In sim every servo then meets the section 9 gates (our build, nominal pack: right knee 9.9 % under 6.0 V at 28 mOhm, VERIFICATION.md E6b; 10.2 % at 29 mOhm, sim/POWER_PATH_SIM.md 'As built').
  - Warn: over 28 up to 40 mOhm per conductor. The build goes on. The sim predicts a knee over 10 % under 6.0 V in section 9 at a nominal pack (at 40 mOhm the right knee 13.4 %, the ankles 8.0 %; our build), so the section 9 walking log decides, and the 3S plan (sim/POWER_FIX.md option 6) is made ready for Ahmed.
  - Fail: over 40 mOhm per conductor. A contact or crimp is worse than any corner we modelled as typical: find it and fix it before the full assembly.
- With a 3S bus (held by D23) a hop passes up to 100 mOhm. Without the second feeds no hop value passes section 9 (15.4 % at 17.6 mOhm). The ankles alone (sim: the ankle gate holds to about 60 mOhm) are no longer the limit; the knees and the head roll are.
- 22 AWG made leads (BOM contingency X1/X2) cut the ankle time under 6 V by only 2 to 5 points: use them if the kit cables fail the B17 length test fit, not for voltage.
- Twin (twin/electromech/ELECTROMECH_TWIN.md, closed loop, our 2.65 kg build, sim pass only; a prediction, not a gate): from 28 to 40 mOhm per conductor the walk speed at a nominal pack moves by under 1 % (0.702 to 0.700 of the command) and the knee time under 6.0 V goes from 4.5 / 4.8 to 7.2 / 7.8 % (left / right). The open-loop numbers above are the conservative ones and stay the gate.

## 7. First servo (test order TO1)
Bench supply at 7.4 V, limit 3 A, servo alone.
1. configure_motor.py with the ID it will have; then read back registers 41, 44, 46, 84, 85, 86 and the firmware version. Pass: 85 = 0 (UPSTREAM_ISSUES.md G1), firmware written down. Use bench/first_servo_check.py --configure ID --fix-config; power-cycle and re-read register 85 (the stock configure script can lose writes, software/SOFTWARE.md section 3).
2. No-load speed: a 2 rad step and a 1.5 Hz 0.4 rad sine (SERVO_DEEP_DIVE.md section 6). Pass: about 5 rad/s or more at 7.4 V.
3. Thermal (D2 and its 2026-11-03 note; twin/thermal/THERMAL_TWIN.md, every twin number here sim pass only). Horn fixed against a rigid stop, 7.2 V, room temperature written down. Read registers 13 (Max Temperature Limit) and 19 (Unload Condition) once. Log Present Temperature (63), Present Current (69), Present Load (60) and Present Voltage (62) every 2 s; if a thermocouple is at hand, tape it to the largest free face of the case and log it too. Step A: command past the stop so that Present Current reads 0.9 A and hold until the reading rises less than 0.5 C in 5 minutes (twin, central: about 57 C after 30 minutes, time constant about 6 minutes, about 12 K/W; twin/thermal/to1_predict.json). Step B: 1.2 A until the reading is 60 C or 15 minutes have passed (twin: held on, the 70 C cut comes at about 9 minutes). Do not hold 1.5 A: the twin puts the 70 C cut at about 4 minutes (1 to 5 over its corners). From step A: the stall loop resistance R = duty x Present Voltage / Present Current, with the duty from Present Load (about 2.8 ohm cold; 2.6 to 2.9 ohm in the twin), whose rise gives the winding temperature (copper +0.393 % per C); thermal resistance = (reading at the end - room) / (current^2 x R + 0.15 W of electronics); time constant = time to 63 % of the rise; the winding estimate, the reading and the thermocouple together give where the sensor sits. Put the three values into twin/thermal/twin_thermal.py and re-run it. Pass: the re-run twin keeps every knee at least 10 C under the 70 C cut (and under the register 13 limit, if that reads lower) through a full charge of the session the lab runs first (proposed: stand and walk 50/50 at a 32 C room). If it does not pass: first the thermal guard with rests or a duty limit (THERMAL_TWIN.md section 6); SERVO_UPGRADE_OPTIONS.md only if those are not enough (a contingency; buying needs Ahmed's OK). If the servo cuts its torque during a hold, write down when and at what reading. bench/first_servo_check.py --thermal still runs the earlier 1.5 Nm hold with its own gate (limit minus 10 C): use its log, not its gate, until it is updated. How the servo releases its 70 C cut is not published (BLOCKER), and the live twin in the shared lab gym (overlay gym/overlays/duck) needs it: if the hold of step B reaches the cut, keep the same goal position at 50 Hz and log registers 40, 63, 65 and 69 every 0.2 s for 2 minutes: at what reading the torque comes back, and whether a goal write brings it back. The twin assumes it comes back at 70 C with no hysteresis; then a walking duck's knee drops out every 2 to 8 s and the duck falls 0.8 to 115 s after the first cut (sim pass only). Risk: one servo was reported damaged over 70 C (twin/thermal/THERMAL_TWIN.md source R3); do it on a spare unit or skip it.
4. Note: the servo cuts its torque after more than 2 A for 2 s by default (Feetech spec via the parts check). Do not mistake this for a fault.
5. Bus volts response (twin/electromech/ELECTROMECH_TWIN.md, its largest open input, HARDWARE_AUDIT B39; sim pass only for the twin numbers). Servo at P 32, horn on the 7.3 rig, goal 0.05 rad past the stop. Read Present Load (register 60, output duty) and Present Current (69) for 10 s with the bench supply at 7.4 V, then at 6.0 V. Same duty and the current lower by about 6.0 / 7.4: the firmware does not compensate (the BAM model the twin uses). Duty up by about 1.23 x and the same current: it compensates (the twin's 'comp' case: at a nominal pack the walk keeps 0.859 of the command, 95 % of the trained speed, against 0.706, and the knees spend 3 to 4 x longer under 6.0 V). Then repeat 7.2 at 6.0 V: expected no-load speed 4.4 to 4.6 rad/s (C001 data sheet 42 RPM, model 4.56 rad/s). Write both readings into history/; they set the twin's servo mode (twin/electromech/servo_sts3215.py, bam or comp).
